# Coverage: ISO 25010 Software Quality Model

**Source:** https://iso25000.com/en/iso-25000-standards/iso-25010
**Mapped against spec version:** 0.3.0
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-18

Mapped against the eight product quality characteristics of ISO/IEC
25010:2011 (the 2023 revision reorganizes them and adds Safety; the mapping
below holds for both, with Safety noted at the end). This mapping reads
differently from the checklist frameworks: ISO 25010 describes qualities a
product should *have*, while this assessment evaluates practices a team
*performs*. The table therefore answers: "which of our items produce or
verify evidence for this quality?" The assessment covers 5 of 8
characteristics in full and 3 partially.

| # | Characteristic (sub-characteristics) | Our item(s) | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Functional Suitability (completeness, correctness, appropriateness) | 1.4 Functional and Non-Functional Requirements, 1.6 User Research & Feedback Loops, 5.5 Project Testing, 4.1 Relevance of Codebase | ✅ Full |
| 2 | Performance Efficiency (time behaviour, resource use, capacity) | 3.5 Scalability & Performance, 5.7 Optimizations | ✅ Full |
| 3 | Compatibility (co-existence, interoperability) | 3.8 APIs & Integrations | ⚠️ Partial |
| 4 | Usability (learnability, operability, accessibility, error protection) | 1.6 User Research & Feedback Loops, 1.8 Compliance & Legal Requirements (accessibility) | ⚠️ Partial |
| 5 | Reliability (maturity, availability, fault tolerance, recoverability) | 3.6 Resilience & Fault Tolerance, 5.10 Recovery Plan, 5.12 Monitoring & Alerting Quality | ✅ Full |
| 6 | Security (confidentiality, integrity, accountability, authenticity) | Category 6 (6.1–6.10) | ✅ Full |
| 7 | Maintainability (modularity, reusability, analysability, modifiability, testability) | 3.4 Loose Coupling / High Cohesion, 4.2 Patterns, 4.3 Code Style, 4.6 Technical Debt, 4.8 Unit Tests, 4.11 Documentation & Domain Language | ✅ Full |
| 8 | Portability (adaptability, installability, replaceability) | 5.2 Project Installation, 5.3 Project Launch, 5.14 Infrastructure as Code | ⚠️ Partial |

## Notes on partial coverage

- **Compatibility.** Interoperability is well covered by 3.8 (contracts,
  versioning, integration testing), but co-existence — behaving well while
  sharing an environment with other software — is never asked. Minor in
  practice; it matters mostly for installed/embedded software.
- **Usability.** The largest real gap in the whole coverage series. 1.6
  covers whether user feedback flows back into the product, and 1.8 catches
  accessibility as a compliance obligation, but no item assesses UX practice
  itself: usability testing, design review, error-message quality,
  learnability. A dedicated "UX & Accessibility" item (category 1) is the
  strongest candidate this series has produced for a future MINOR release.
- **Portability.** Installability is covered (5.2, 5.3) and IaC (5.14) makes
  environments reproducible, but adaptability (moving to a different
  platform/cloud) and replaceability are not asked. Deliberately thin: for
  most teams this is a low-importance item, and the importance rating
  mechanism handles that better than omitting the quality entirely.

## Note on ISO 25010:2023

The 2023 revision adds **Safety** (harm avoidance) as a ninth
characteristic. It is not covered: no item assesses functional safety.
Deliberate for now — safety-critical domains (medical, automotive,
industrial) have dedicated standards (IEC 61508, ISO 26262) that a generic
maturity assessment should not imitate. Teams in those domains should record
this under the category "Other" items.

## Summary judgment

The practices assessed here produce evidence for every 2011 characteristic
except parts of usability. ISO 25010 works well as a complement: use it to
name the qualities a product needs, then use this assessment to check
whether the practices that deliver those qualities exist and how mature they
are.
