# Coverage: SPACE Developer Productivity Framework

**Source:** [The SPACE of Developer Productivity: There's more to it than you think (ACM Queue, 2021)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3454122.3454124)
**Mapped against spec version:** 0.2.0
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-20

SPACE describes developer productivity through five connected dimensions:
Satisfaction and well-being, Performance, Activity, Communication and
collaboration, and Efficiency and flow. The framework cautions against
reducing productivity to one metric or dimension. This assessment covers
engineering practices and team context that can provide evidence for these
dimensions, but it is not a SPACE productivity measurement system. All five
dimensions are **Partial**.

## Notes on partial and missing coverage

- **Satisfaction and well-being.** Items 7.1, 7.4, and 7.5 ask about working
  conditions, growth, and team health. They do not measure developer
  satisfaction, autonomy, burnout, or well-being systematically over time.
- **Performance.** Product outcomes, testing, and operational quality provide
  related evidence. The assessment does not define a developer-performance
  measure or connect individual contributions to outcomes; it should not be
  used to rank people.
- **Activity.** Task tracking, code review, source control, and releases make
  work visible. The assessment does not prescribe activity counts, and
  commit, pull request, or ticket counts alone are not evidence of productivity.
- **Communication and collaboration.** The assessment asks about communication
  channels, cross-team work, reviews, stakeholder engagement, and remote
  collaboration. It does not evaluate the full range of informal collaboration
  or knowledge-sharing that affects software work.
- **Efficiency and flow.** Setup, build, automation, task management, and
  technical debt can reveal friction. The assessment does not measure wait
  time, interruptions, cognitive load, or uninterrupted flow directly.
- **Use multiple dimensions.** The five dimensions are interrelated. Use
  evidence from several dimensions and interpret measures in the team's
  context; do not substitute a single activity or speed measure for
  productivity as a whole.

The assessment's importance-weighted item scores are not SPACE scores or
productivity measures. The frameworks have different purposes, constructs, and
rating methods; no direct score comparison or statistical correlation is
claimed.
